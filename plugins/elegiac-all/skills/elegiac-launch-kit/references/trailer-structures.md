# Trailer & Teaser Structures

Beat templates for trailer kits. A trailer is an argument for watching, not a summary — sell the feeling, protect the plot.

## The 3-act theatrical trailer (90–150s)

| Act | Length | Job | Beats |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — World | ~30% | Establish tone, place, protagonist normal | Open on atmosphere (logo-free), 2-3 world shots, protagonist introduced doing something characteristic, first line of dialogue |
| 2 — Rupture | ~40% | The problem arrives; stakes escalate | Inciting image, rhythm accelerates, shots shorten, music shifts/builds, montage of escalating moments, one strong dialogue exchange |
| 3 — Promise | ~30% | Money shots + title | Music drops out (the breath), biggest image, rapid-fire climax montage (no resolutions shown), TITLE CARD, button |

**The button:** one final beat *after* the title — a joke, a scare, or a held look. Always include one; it's what people remember.

## The 30s teaser

Atmosphere → single unsettling/enticing image → one line (VO or dialogue) → title → date. No plot. A teaser sells a *question*; the trailer sells the *promise*.

## The 60s festival/indie cut

World (15s) → protagonist + want (15s) → texture montage of 5-8 striking images with one dialogue line laid across (20s) → title + laurels + date (10s). Festival cuts privilege image-making over plot — pick the 8 most cinematic frames you have.

## Rhythm rules

- Shot lengths *contract* across the trailer: 3-4s opens, sub-1s by the climax montage.
- Insert 2 silences ("breaths"): before act 3, and before the title.
- Cut on motion or music accents; when generating animatic-style trailers, request shot durations explicitly per beat.

## Title cards

- 2-4 cards max before the main title ("FROM …", "THIS [SEASON]", premise card). One thought per card, ≤6 words.
- Card style inherits the key art's title treatment (see `poster-key-art.md`) — same face, same texture.
- Generate cards as stills with a text-capable model; verify spelling.

## Trailer VO

Three registers — pick one, don't mix: **Premise VO** ("In a world where…", earnest, mostly genre work now), **Character VO** (lines lifted from the film, intimate), **No VO** (title cards carry it — the modern default).

## Music brief language (for generated score)

Specify: genre anchor + tempo arc + emotional arc + hits. Example: "Cinematic hybrid score, 90s, starts sparse piano and room tone, builds with percussive hits at 0:30 and 0:50, drops to silence at 1:05, full orchestral/synth climax to 1:25, hard out." Ask for stems-style separation only if mixing externally.

## Genre signatures

| Genre | Open with | Rhythm | Color/music tells |
|---|---|---|---|
| Horror | Normalcy too quiet | Long holds → violent acceleration; silence before scares | Desaturated; sub-bass risers, one shrill motif |
| Comedy | A complete joke (setup+punch) | Steady; jokes need air | Bright; needle-drop song |
| Drama / festival | A face | Even, patient | Naturalistic; sparse piano/strings |
| Action | Cold-open stunt | Fast early and faster late | Teal/orange; percussion + bass drops |
| Sci-fi | Scale (one vast image) | Measured awe → overwhelm | Cold palette; synth pads → braams |
| Thriller | A secret being kept | Tightening spiral | High contrast; ticking textures |
