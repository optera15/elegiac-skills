# Ad Frameworks (variants, hooks, UGC scripts, disclosure)

How to make ad variants that test something, and script structures for UGC-style spots.

## Variant axes — vary the message, not just the crop

A useful A/B set holds everything constant except ONE axis:

| Axis | Variants look like |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | Problem-first ("Still doing X by hand?") vs outcome-first ("Done in 4 minutes") vs curiosity ("We weren't going to share this") vs social proof ("12,000 editors switched") |
| **Benefit** | Time saved vs money saved vs status/identity vs ease |
| **Emotion** | Aspirational vs relief/pain-killer vs humor vs FOMO |
| **Format** | Talking presenter vs product demo vs text-overlay-only vs lifestyle vignette |
| **CTA** | "Shop now" vs "Learn more" vs offer-led ("First month free") |

Plan sets as: 1 control + 3-4 single-axis challengers. Label every variant with its axis ("Hook B: outcome-first") so results are interpretable. Resize/platform versions of the same message are *placements*, not variants — see `platform-specs.md`.

## Hook bank (first 2 seconds, 9:16)

Pattern interrupts that survive sound-off: an unexpected visual contradiction; text overlay posing a direct question; mid-action start (no intro); a bold claim with a number; the product failing→succeeding. Burn the hook as on-screen text too — most feed viewing is muted.

## UGC script skeletons (15-30s)

**Problem → Discovery → Proof → CTA** (the workhorse):
1. Hook: the problem, dramatized, 2s
2. "Then I found…" product intro in-hand, 4s
3. Proof: one demo moment or one concrete result, 6s
4. CTA + offer, 3s

**Testimonial:** claim → who-I-am one-liner → before/after → "would I buy it again?" → CTA.

**How-to:** end-result first (the hook is the payoff) → 3 numbered steps → product as the enabler of step 2 → CTA.

Write UGC lines as speech, not copy: contractions, false starts allowed, ≤12 words a sentence. Direct-address eyeline ("talking to a friend on FaceTime").

## UGC visual grammar

Handheld micro-shake, available light, real-room background with believable clutter, phone-native 9:16, MCU direct address, jump cuts between sentences. Imperfection is the aesthetic — "too clean" reads as an ad and gets scrolled.

## Static ad anatomy (1:1 / 4:5)

One visual subject + ≤6-word headline + brand mark + CTA chip. The image carries the benefit; the headline names it; nothing else. Text-capable model required for overlays (see `model-catalog.md`).

## Disclosure & claims

- Synthetic presenters/voices in ads: disclose where platform policy requires; never present a synthetic person as a real customer giving a real testimonial.
- Concrete claims ("saves 3 hours") must come from the user — never invent stats, results, or endorsements.
- Before/after imagery for health/beauty/finance has platform-specific restrictions; flag rather than assume.
